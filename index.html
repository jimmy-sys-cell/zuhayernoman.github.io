01 — About

Who I Am

I'm a Chemical Engineering student at Dalhousie University with a focused interest in materials engineering and the thermodynamic, electrochemical, and structural properties of energy storage and conversion systems. My work spans both renewable and non-renewable energy domains — from the solid-state chemistry of intercalation materials and ionic transport mechanisms in next-generation cell chemistries, to the mechanical and thermal management challenges inherent in high-density energy systems.

I approach engineering problems at multiple scales: from characterising phase transformations and degradation kinetics at the material level, to designing precision mechanical assemblies and instrumented test infrastructure at the systems level. I'm equally comfortable running controlled electrochemical experiments, performing force and pressure calculations, or processing large multivariate datasets to extract actionable performance trends.

My longer-term interests lie at the intersection of material science and sustainable energy — understanding how the molecular architecture of electrode, electrolyte, and structural materials can be engineered to unlock safer, more efficient, and more scalable energy technologies across the full spectrum of applications.
